Fusionism

Also known as: Conservative Fusionism, Libertarian Conservatism

Formulated by Frank S. Meyer (1962)
menu_book From In Defense of Freedom: A Conservative Credo

Definition

A political philosophy that merges the libertarian defense of individual liberty and free markets with the traditionalist defense of virtue, religion and moral order. Developed by Frank S. Meyer, an editor of National Review, and set out in In Defense of Freedom (1962), it holds that the state's role is to secure freedom, because virtue only has moral value when freely chosen and cannot be imposed by coercion. Libertarians get limited government, traditionalists get the cultural ends that free people are expected to pursue. Fusionism became the dominant outlook of the American right through the Reagan years, and critics on both sides still contest it: traditionalists accuse it of hollowing out moral order, libertarians of subordinating liberty to tradition. Murray Rothbard went further, arguing in The Fusionist as Libertarian Manqué (1981) that fusionism does not really exist: on freedom and virtue, individualism and order, Meyer was simply a libertarian, and the fusion was a myth meant to hold a movement together.
